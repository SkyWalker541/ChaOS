Get started

You need a Brick or Brick Hammer, a microSD card, and a computer just this once.

  1. Put ChaOS on the card

    ChaOS Installer (macOS, Windows, Linux) downloads the latest ChaOS and writes it to the card. It also updates a card that already has ChaOS or TortOS, keeping everything on it.

    Or by hand

    Download ChaOS-v<version>-brick.img.xz from Releases and write it with Raspberry Pi Imager or balenaEtcher. This erases the card.

  2. Turn it on

    Put the card in and power on. The first boot takes a little longer while the rest of the card becomes space for games, music and saves. The logo's pixels fly in, then the shelf.

  3. Set the clock

    Press MENU on the systems row, then Date & Time. Left and right change each row; set your time zone. Wi-Fi keeps the clock right after that.

  4. Join Wi-Fi

    MENU > Wi-Fi, pick your network, and type the password on the on-screen keyboard. Y rescans; X forgets a network.

  5. Add games

    MENU > ChaOS Transfer shows an address and a PIN. Open the address in a browser on your phone or computer, type the PIN, and drag games into their console's folder. Or copy them straight onto the card from a computer, into Roms/<console>.

  6. Get the covers

    MENU > Box Art fetches every missing cover over Wi-Fi. Then pick a console and press A on a game.

Playing games

  • Instant start. The emulator is already running, so nothing loads between you and the game.
  • Resume. Quit from the menu or press power: the game is saved on the way out and continues from that moment next time.
  • Six save slots per game, each with a picture of where you were, from Save and Load in the in-game menu.
  • Fast forward with MENU+R1: 2×, 3×, 4×, then normal. Silent while fast; heavy SNES and GBA games top out around 2.5×.
  • Rewind ChaOS: turn it on in a console's menu, then hold MENU+L1 to step back through the last minutes. It costs a little speed, so it's off until you want it.
  • Button mapping ChaOS: a console's Controls shows every button and lets you change it, for the console or just one game, with turbo on any button.
  • Turbo: X and Y are turbo A and B on the consoles with two face buttons; L2/R2 are turbo L and R on Game Boy Advance.
  • Screenshots ChaOS with MENU+X, looked at full screen from Screenshots in the in-game menu. They're PNGs in Screenshots/<console>/<game>/.
  • RetroAchievements: sign in under MENU > RetroAchievements. Unlocks pop up as you play; ones earned offline are sent later.

Games come first ChaOS

Nothing of ChaOS's own runs while you play. An update that was downloading pauses and carries on afterwards, its progress shown on the shelf.

The battery to the end

The game is saved and the Brick turns off when the battery is truly empty, judged by its voltage: about half an hour past the gauge's 2%. A red dot blinks for the last stretch.

Play Time

MENU > Play Time: per game or per console, for today, the week, month, year or all time. Y switches; the bottom line has launches, longest session and last played.

Cheats ChaOS

Game Genie, Action Replay, GameShark and Code Breaker codes, and RetroArch's memory cheats, from the in-game menu.

  1. In a game, press MENU and choose Cheats. It's there for any game with cheats to get.
  2. Get Cheats finds the game in libretro's cheat database over Wi-Fi and lists its cheat files, best match first. Show All lists the console's whole collection, with L2/R2 to jump by letter.
  3. Each cheat has a switch. A turns it on or off, and it takes effect in the game at once.
  • Your choices are kept per game and come back next time you play it.
  • Your own cheats: put RetroArch .cht files in Cheats/<console>/, named like the game, or send them with ChaOS Transfer.
  • Works on NES, SNES, Genesis, Master System, Game Gear, TurboGrafx-16 and the Game Boys. Neo Geo Pocket takes memory cheats only.
!

Cheats and achievements

RetroAchievements doesn't count unlocks made with cheats. The moment a cheat goes on, achievements pause for the rest of that game, and the in-game menu says Paused. Turn the cheats off and start the game again to earn them.

Cheats are a list kept by the community, so some may not work with every version of a game. Cheats that ask for a number of your own (shown as Can't use) aren't offered.

Shader support ChaOS

ChaOS adds shaders to every console: CRT, LCD and PT SkyWalker541 looks, each with settings to tune, drawn by the Brick's CPU in ChaOS-diatom. Find the Shader row in a console's menu (MENU on its shelf) or in the in-game menu.

  • Left and right switch it on and off; A opens its settings.
  • See it before you choose it. In a game, the paused picture shows each setting as you scroll. Hold Y to hide the menu and look at the whole screen.
  • Reset to Recommended puts back the shader's own settings.
  • Settings are kept per console, and survive updates.

Scanlines for speed

Scanlines costs about a tenth of crt-lottes-fast. If a heavy SNES game slows down with the CRT, try it.

Curvature costs

The CRT starts flat. Curvature works, but takes about twice the CPU, so check the speed after turning it on.

PT's grid wants Integer

PT's Pixel Effect follows the game's pixels, so it's drawn in Display Mode Integer only. For a grid in Aspect or Stretch, use lcd3x.

PT sets the emulator for you. With PT on, ChaOS sets mGBA the way the shader is tuned for, by its Game System: color correction and frame blending off and a plain grayscale palette for Game Boy, GBC color correction for Game Boy Color, GBA color correction with Smart blending for GBA SP and LCD ghosting for the original GBA. Turn PT off and they go back. It all changes at once, in the in-game menu too.

The speed check ChaOS

In a game, the Shader menu says whether the game keeps full speed with the shader as you've set it. It's measured from the frames you actually play, not guessed.

Full Speed
The whole frame takes under 85% of the time a frame has. Room to spare.
Near the Limit
It fits, just. A busy scene may slow it down.
Too Slow
The game can't keep up. Lighten the shader or try another style.
(est.)
An estimate: the new settings haven't been played with yet.
  • Shader Time is the shader's share of each frame. Frame Time is the whole frame's work, against the time a frame has (16.7 ms at 60 fps).
  • If Frame Time is high but Shader Time small, the console's emulator is the slow part, not the shader.
!

Get a good reading

The check averages every frame played since the shader was last changed, and needs at least half a second of play before it stops estimating. For a reading you can trust:

  1. Set the shader the way you like it, then close the menu.
  2. Play for a minute or two, ideally through a busy part of the game: lots of sprites, scrolling, effects. Quiet title screens flatter it.
  3. Open the in-game menu and the Shader page again. Without (est.), the reading is real.

Change any setting and the count starts over, so check again after each change you want to judge.

Display Mode and overlays ChaOS

  • Display Mode per console: Integer (whole-number scaling, every pixel the same size), Aspect (as large as the game's shape allows) or Stretch (the whole screen). Every shader works in all three. Changed in a game, it changes as you press.
  • Overlays: frames drawn around the picture. Included for Game Boy, Game Boy Color and Game Boy Advance (by SkyWalker541 and KrutzOtrem), Game Gear and Neo Geo Pocket Color (by KrutzOtrem).
  • Add your own: every console has a folder in Overlays/. Use 1024×768 PNGs with a transparent window for the game. The NextUI Community Overlays has many more.
  • The included overlays are framed for Integer, and come back after an update if missing. Yours are never touched.
  • Crop Overscan for NES, TurboGrafx-16 and Master System: trims the edges an old TV hid, where some games leave flickering tiles or a blank column. In the console's menu and the in-game menu; in a game it shows as soon as you go back to it.

Shelves and themes

  • Four UI Themes: Plain Jane (a drawn card per console), Fancy Pants (the consoles themselves, rendered in 3D), and ChaOS's own Pixel Cards and Pixel Names ChaOS, where each console is its name in the logo's pixel letters, last word in the console's colour.
  • Pixel animation ChaOS: the name you land on flies together pixel by pixel. Transition Speed sets how fast (Slow, Normal, Fast, or Static for none), separately for each direction.
  • Three UI Directions: Horizontal, Vertical, or Cubic, a cube where up and down change console and left and right change game. Pixel Cards has no Cubic; Pixel Names is Horizontal only.
  • Game List View ChaOS: a console's games as a scrolling list in the menus' style instead of cards.
  • Favorites (Y on a game, and it says so) and Recently Played ChaOS (your last twelve games, every console) are shelves at the front, either one hideable. Up to 20 shelves.
  • Letter scrubbing ChaOS: L2/R2 jump to the previous or next letter on any game shelf, in every theme, list and Cubic, with the letter shown big. Hold the d-pad and after two seconds it shows the letter you're passing.
  • Sort By per console: Name, Play Time, Last Played or Recently Added. Game info on X: synopsis, year, genre and achievements.

Box art

  • MENU > Box Art fetches every missing cover over Wi-Fi from libretro's collection, by file name or, where none matches, by the ROM's checksum. A console's menu does just that console.
  • For one game ChaOS: X on it, then Get Box Art or Replace Box Art: Find Automatically, Search itch.io (where most new homebrew lives), or Use a Screenshot of your own. Left and right look through them; A uses one.
  • From your phone ChaOS: in ChaOS Transfer, open a console's folder and press Cover beside a game. Send a photo or saved picture, or give a link: a picture, an itch.io page, or a Google Images result.
  • Covers you put in Roms/<console>/.media/<game>.png yourself are never touched. A game without one shows its last moment of play, or a card with its name.

ChaOS Transfer and ChaOS Wire ChaOS

  • ChaOS Transfer, over Wi-Fi: MENU > ChaOS Transfer, open the address it shows in any browser, and type the PIN (new every time).
  • Drag in games, music, audiobooks, BIOS files and overlays; get at your saves, save states and screenshots; set covers from your phone.
  • New games are on the shelf when you leave the Transfer screen.
  • ChaOS Wire: the same page over the USB cable, no Wi-Fi needed (Mac, Linux, Windows 11). Plug in, MENU > ChaOS Wire, open http://10.55.0.2. It can also update ChaOS from the computer.
  • Download logs at the bottom of the first page gives one file with the last ten boots, Wi-Fi, headset and account names masked.

Ports with PortMaster ChaOS

PC games rebuilt for handhelds, packaged by PortMaster: Cave Story, Doom, OpenTyrian, Spelunky Classic and over a thousand more, most of them free.

  1. MENU > Get PortMaster downloads it over Wi-Fi. Nothing about ports is on the card until you choose this.
  2. A Ports shelf appears. Its first card, PortMaster, opens the catalog.
  3. A on a port installs it, checked against PortMaster's checksum. Its screenshot becomes its cover.
  • May Run on ChaOS lists what the Brick can run, as PortMaster's own site filters it: no second analog stick, 2 GB of memory or OpenGL needed. Show All lists everything. Also Genres, Installed, Updates and Ready to Run Only.
  • Each port shows its screenshot, genre, size, porter, needs, and description (Y reads it all). L2/R2 jump by letter.
  • Runtimes and libraries come with it: Godot, .NET, Java, Pyxel and the rest are fetched and unpacked with the port, and any library it needs that the Brick hasn't got comes from ChaOS's library pack.

Playing a port

SELECT+START quits at once. POWER freezes the port and asks Resume (exactly where it was), Close Port or Power Off. The d-pad works as the left stick too.

Removing

X on a port's page: Keep Saves and Settings (they wait for a reinstall) or Remove Everything. Uninstall PortMaster does the same for all of it.

Updates keep your saves

A port's update unpacks over the old one, so its saves and any game files you copied in stay. Update PortMaster keeps its runtimes and settings.

i

Ports that need your files

Some ports need data from a game you own, like Doom's WAD files. After installing, the port says which files and where they go. Ready to Run Only hides those. If a port stops on a library ChaOS doesn't have, it says which, and each port's last run is logged in .userdata/brick/ports/logs/.

PICO-8, with your own copy ChaOS

PICO-8 is Lexaloffle's fantasy console. ChaOS doesn't include it: if you've bought it, the Brick runs your copy.

  1. From your Lexaloffle account, download the Raspberry Pi version (pico-8_<version>_raspi.zip).
  2. Put the zip in the card's PICO-8 folder, with ChaOS Transfer (it has a PICO-8 place) or a card reader. ChaOS takes out the two files it needs, pico8_64 and pico8.dat. Copying just those two works as well.
  3. A PICO-8 shelf appears. Its first card is Splore, PICO-8's browser of the carts people share, over Wi-Fi.
  • Your own carts, .p8 and .p8.png, go in Roms/PICO-8. A .p8.png cart is its own cover.
  • B and A are PICO-8's O and X; Y/X and L1/R1 are the same two again. START or MENU opens PICO-8's pause menu. Hold SELECT+START to quit; POWER works as it does in a port.
  • Its settings, cart saves and Splore favourites are kept in ChaOS's userdata and stay through updates. Play time counts as it does for any game.
  • Splore's downloads go through curl, as spruceUI does it, since the Brick's own wget can't reach https.

RomM ChaOS

RomM is a game library you run on your own computer or NAS. MENU > ROM Management > RomM connects the Brick to it over Wi-Fi.

  • Sign in: set Server to its address, like nas.local:8080, then Sign In. On RomM 5 the Brick shows a short code to approve in RomM's web page; on RomM 4, make a pairing code in RomM (your profile, Client API Tokens) and type it in. Only a device token is kept, never a password.
  • Games: your library by console, with covers. A downloads a game onto its shelf with RomM's cover as its box art. A game in several files comes as a folder.
  • Sync Saves: RomM's own save sync for the games you got from it. A save the server replaces is backed up first to Saves/.romm-backup; when both changed, yours goes up beside the server's, so nothing is lost. It runs only when you choose it.
  • BIOS: the firmware RomM keeps for ChaOS's consoles, into Bios/.

Syncthing ChaOS

Syncthing keeps folders the same on your devices, over your own network, with no server in between: the Brick's saves with RetroArch on your computer, its games with another Brick. MENU > ROM Management > Syncthing.

  1. Get Syncthing downloads it (about 13 MB) from Syncthing's own release, checked against its published checksum, and turns it on.
  2. On a computer running Syncthing, choose Add Remote Device. The Brick is in the list of nearby devices; pick it.
  3. On the Brick, "<computer> wants to pair" appears. A pairs, X ignores.
  4. The computer is offered the Brick's shared folders and asks where to keep each.
  • Saves (saves and save states), Games (all of Roms/) and BIOS each switch on and off, and say Up to date or how far along they are. Saves is on to begin with. A folder that's on goes to every paired device, and two Bricks share the same folders with no setting up.
  • Nothing runs while you play. Syncthing is stopped outright for every game, port and PICO-8 cart, and catches up when you're back on the shelf. There it runs at low priority.
  • Web Page shows the Brick's address and password for Syncthing's own page in a browser, for everything else: ignore patterns, file versions, folders of your own.
  • Its keys, settings and database stay in ChaOS's userdata, so updates keep your pairings.

Muse: music and audiobooks

  • SELECT opens Muse from anywhere but a running game (the in-game menu counts), and closes it again. Music keeps playing everywhere; a game goes quiet while it plays and comes back when you pause.
  • Music in Music/<artist>/<album>/. Loose album folders are filed by their tags; loose songs go to Singles.
  • Audiobooks in Audiobooks/, one M4B or a folder of files per book. A book remembers its place and its own speed (0.75× to 2×), and says Finished at the end.
  • MP3, M4A, M4B, MP4, AAC, FLAC, Ogg, Opus and WAV. Play modes: in order, repeat all, repeat one, shuffle.
  • Album Art in Muse's menu fetches covers from MusicBrainz for albums without one.
  • Lock Screen: hold F1+F2 to switch the screen off and lock the buttons while it plays: about 10 hours of listening instead of 6. Volume, power and headset buttons still work.

Sound and Bluetooth

  • Bluetooth headphones: MENU > Bluetooth, Y to search, A to pair, X to forget. They reconnect by themselves, even mid-game. The volume buttons reach them, and their play, pause and skip buttons control Muse.
  • Audio Output: Auto (cable, then Bluetooth, then speaker) or Speaker.
  • Audio Channels: stereo, mono, mono left or mono right, for hearing with one ear.
  • Night Mode ChaOS: eight volume steps below the quietest, for playing in bed. Turning it on or off keeps the loudness you have.
  • Wired headphones get their own volume range, so plugging in never jumps loud.

Look and settings

  • Appearance ChaOS on one page: UI Theme, UI Direction, Transition Speed, Game List View, Menu Color, and Show Favorites, Show Recently Played and Show Muse.
  • Every menu says what its rows do ChaOS, in a line at the bottom.
  • The clock and battery ChaOS in every menu's heading. MENU > Date & Time sets the zone, 12- or 24-hour, and the battery as an icon, a percent or both. Wi-Fi keeps the clock right.
  • Boot and power-off ChaOS: the logo's pixels fly in, and fly out again, like the top of this page.
  • Auto Off after a while without a button (not while music plays or it's charging).
  • About ChaOS: version, the TortOS it's built on, free space on the card, address, battery, time awake.

Updates

  • MENU > Updates, over Wi-Fi. Your games, music, saves, settings and ports always stay.
  • When the Brick joins Wi-Fi it checks once and says if there's a newer ChaOS. It never installs by itself.
  • What's New shows what a release changes. Update downloads only what changed when it can.
  • Full Install is always offered, and repairs a card whose files have gone wrong.
  • Needs the charger or a quarter charge. If the power goes out, the Brick finishes on the next start.
  • Or update over the cable with ChaOS Wire, or with ChaOS Installer on a computer.

FAQ

My games don't show up.

Check the folder name against the console table, spelled exactly, and that the file type is listed. Games sent over ChaOS Transfer appear when you leave its screen; games copied another way while the Brick is on appear after Rescan Folder in the console's menu, or a restart.

Will an update or reinstall lose my saves?

No. Updates over Wi-Fi, ChaOS Wire and ChaOS Installer's Update all keep your games, music, saves, settings, ports and port saves. Only writing the image by hand erases the card.

A port won't start, or shows a black screen.

Check it's under May Run on ChaOS. If ChaOS names a missing library, it may come in a later library pack. Otherwise send the port's log from .userdata/brick/ports/logs/.

Something went wrong. How do I send the logs?

Open MENU > ChaOS Transfer, open its address in a browser, and click Download logs. Then open an issue with it attached.

Is PICO-8 included?

No. PICO-8 is sold by Lexaloffle, and ChaOS runs your own copy: its Raspberry Pi download, put in the card's PICO-8 folder. See PICO-8.

Should I use RomM or Syncthing for saves?

Either. RomM's save sync is for games you got from your RomM server, and runs when you choose it. Syncthing keeps whole folders the same on your devices all the time, with or without a server. Using both on the same saves isn't needed.

How do I go back to the Brick's own system?

Take the card out. ChaOS changes nothing on the Brick itself. Everything ChaOS keeps, Wi-Fi passwords and Bluetooth pairings included, is on the card, so reformat it before you give it away.

TortOS, Diatom and plastron

by Eric Reinsmidt. ChaOS is a fork of TortOS: the launcher, menus, Muse, file transfer, box art, RetroAchievements and the rest of what makes it work on the Brick are Eric's. Games run in ChaOS-diatom, a fork of his Diatom, on his plastron system.

ChaOS

by SkyWalker541: the shaders' ports and PT SkyWalker541, the pixel themes, logo and animations, PortMaster, RomM, Syncthing and PICO-8 support, and the rest marked ChaOS above.

Shaders

crt-lottes-fast by Timothy Lottes, adapted by hunterk. lcd3x by Gigaherz. Scanlines after RiskyJumps. PT SkyWalker541 uses Hash without Sine by David Hoskins, and its dot mode is inspired by Themaister's.

Emulator cores

FCEUmm, Snes9x 2010, Genesis Plus GX, Beetle PCE Fast, Beetle NeoPop and mGBA, from libretro, each its authors' work. Snes9x 2010 and Genesis Plus GX forbid commercial use, so ChaOS may never be sold.

Overlays and art

Overlays by SkyWalker541 (CC0) and KrutzOtrem (MIT), via NextUI Community Overlays. Fancy Pants console photographs by Evan Amos. Josefin Sans by Santiago Orozco (OFL).

Ports and services

PortMaster and its porters; minui-portmaster by ben16w. RomM and its client Grout. Syncthing (MPL-2.0), downloaded from its own releases, not part of ChaOS. PICO-8 by Lexaloffle Games, not included; the way its Splore downloads with curl is spruceUI's. RetroAchievements, libretro thumbnails, libretro's cheat database (CC BY-SA 4.0), MusicBrainz and the Cover Art Archive, and itch.io (with NextUI-Itchio-Pak by Christophe Vanlancker).

Libraries

SDL2 and its mixer, net and gfx libraries, OpenAL Soft, FluidSynth, libsndfile, Xiph's Ogg, Vorbis, FLAC and Opus, GNU bash, squashfs-tools, and the port library pack's cairo, pixman, fontconfig, GMP, libtheora and libevdev, each under its own licence.

ChaOS is TortOS by Eric Reinsmidt, with ChaOS's changes by SkyWalker541. Games run in ChaOS-diatom; ports come from PortMaster.
